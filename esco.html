How the RWA ESCO NFT flow works

The company signs ESCO contracts, installs energy-saving equipment using bank credit, and issues NFTs that give holders a share of the verified ESCO performance. No double ownership, no confusion: equipment stays on the company balance sheet, rewards flow to NFT holders.

Step 1

ESCO contract signed

The ESCO company signs a performance-based contract with an industrial client (factory, building, etc.), committing to reduce energy consumption and share savings.

Real-world
Step 2

Installation via bank credit

The company finances equipment through unsecured credit or corporate loans (e.g. 6% interest). The physical hardware remains 100% owned by the ESCO company.

No double ownership
Step 3

Stablecoin-based NFT sale

In parallel, RWA NFTs are issued and sold for stablecoins. Each NFT represents the right to receive performance-based rewards from the ESCO project – not legal ownership of the equipment.

Stablecoins in
Step 4

Rewards 1–2 times per year

Based on verified annual savings, a portion of ESCO profit is distributed to NFT holders on chain, targeting around 12% reward rate, while the company maintains a healthy margin after loan interest.

Equipment

Core energy-saving equipment we deploy

The ESCO projects are not abstract. They are powered by proven energy-saving technologies with short payback periods in Korea. Below are two key solutions used in our RWA pipeline: Ecomech and Savenet.

Ecomech – ECO series
Typical payback in Korea: within ~2 years

Ecomech is an ECO-type energy-saving solution designed for industrial and building cooling systems. It focuses on improving chiller and cooling tower efficiency by optimizing heat exchange and reducing wasted energy in real operating conditions.

In Korea, typical case studies show a simple payback period of around 2 years, depending on site conditions and energy tariffs.

Ecomech – field installation
Payback in Korea: ~2 years (typical)

This installation view of Ecomech shows how the ECO equipment is integrated into existing cooling infrastructure with minimal disruption. It is suitable for retrofits where quick payback and operational stability are critical for factory managers.

Ecomech projects are typically selected when the client requires short payback and measurable CO₂ reduction from cooling optimization.

Savenet – SUPER series
Typical payback in Korea: within ~3 years

Savenet (SUPER series) is a more advanced energy-saving system that targets deeper reductions in power consumption for cooling and HVAC systems. It often combines control optimization with improved heat rejection and hardware-level enhancements.

In Korean industrial sites, Savenet projects generally show a simple payback of around 3 years, with significant long-term savings over the full equipment lifetime.

Savenet – installed system
Payback in Korea: ~3 years (typical)

This Savenet installation demonstrates how the SUPER system is implemented on site, often as part of a broader ESCO package. It is well-suited for clients aiming at both energy cost reduction and long-term resilience of critical cooling assets.

Savenet is usually proposed when the client can accept a slightly longer payback in exchange for deeper, more durable savings.
